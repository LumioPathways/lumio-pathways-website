What the waiting list means for your family right now

When your child is referred for a neurodevelopmental assessment, you are not at the end of a process. You are at the beginning of one. The referral is acknowledgement that something needs investigating. The waiting list is not a place where nothing happens. It is time. And time can be used.

Most families are not told this. They are given a letter, a waiting list number and very little else. Lumio Pathways was built specifically for this moment — the space between a parent's first concern and a diagnosis, a plan or an EHCP.

Your legal position right now

A diagnosis is not required for your child to receive SEND support at school. It is not required to request an EHCP needs assessment. Under the Children and Families Act 2014, the relevant question is whether your child has special educational needs — not whether they have a diagnosis. You can act now.

Your legal rights while you wait

These are not suggestions. These are statutory rights under the Children and Families Act 2014 and the SEND Code of Practice 2015.

The right to SEND support without a diagnosis

Schools must support children with SEND using a graduated approach — assess, plan, do, review — whether or not a diagnosis exists.

The right to request an EHCP assessment at any time

Under Section 36, Children and Families Act 2014. The local authority must respond within 6 weeks. They cannot refuse without giving reasons.

The right to appeal a refusal

If the local authority refuses to assess or refuses to issue an EHCP, you have the right to appeal to the First-tier Tribunal (SEND) under Section 51.

The right to a copy of all reports

You are entitled to copies of all professional reports about your child. Request them in writing within 5 working days of any appointment.

The right to be involved in EHCP decisions

If an EHCP is issued, you must be given the opportunity to contribute evidence and comment on the draft before it is finalised.

The right to name a school

When an EHCP is issued, you have the right to request a specific school. The local authority must name it unless exceptional circumstances apply.

What to record every day while you wait

The most valuable thing you can do during the waiting period is keep a daily record. Not a diary — a structured log that a clinician can read and a tribunal can reference. Lumio makes this automatic, but here is what matters most.

What to record daily
  • Sleep — bedtime, wake time, how settled, any disturbances
  • Food — what was accepted, what was refused, any sensory reactions
  • Mood and regulation — overall rating, any incidents, what triggered them, what helped
  • School feedback — anything the teacher reported, any incidents at school
  • Activities — which strategies you tried and whether they worked
  • Specific incidents — date, time, what happened before, during and after

After 28 days of consistent logging, Lumio's correlation engine analyses the data and identifies patterns you cannot see day to day. Which foods correlate with poor sleep. Which transitions consistently trigger dysregulation. Which strategies consistently reduce distress. This is the kind of longitudinal evidence that takes clinicians years to gather — and you build it automatically.

What happens at a community paediatric assessment

Knowing what to expect reduces the anxiety of waiting. Here is what a typical community paediatric assessment looks like in England.

Before the appointment

Parent questionnaires and school report

You will usually be asked to complete detailed questionnaires about your child's development and behaviour. The school will be asked to submit a report. Submit your Lumio tracking data and profile as additional evidence.

At the appointment

Clinical interview and observation

The community paediatrician will interview you and observe your child. They may use standardised assessment tools. The appointment typically lasts 60 to 90 minutes. You can bring someone with you for support.

After the appointment

Report and outcome

You are entitled to a copy of the full assessment report. Request it in writing within 5 working days. The outcome may be a diagnosis, a referral for further assessment or a recommendation for specific support.

If the outcome is inconclusive

You can still pursue an EHCP

An inconclusive assessment does not mean your child does not need support. Your Lumio evidence of daily impact remains valid for an EHCP application and a Tribunal appeal.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get SEND support before my child has a diagnosis?
Yes. Schools have a legal duty to support children with SEND under the SEND Code of Practice 2015 whether or not a diagnosis exists. Request a meeting with the SENCO and ask what graduated support is in place for your child.
Can I request an EHCP while waiting for an autism assessment?
Yes. You can request an EHCP needs assessment at any time under Section 36 of the Children and Families Act 2014. A diagnosis is not required. The local authority must tell you within 6 weeks whether they will carry out an assessment.
The school says my child is fine. What do I do?
Many children with neurodevelopmental needs mask in school and struggle at home. This is well documented and clinically recognised. Keep a detailed record of what happens at home and bring it to any school meeting. Ask the SENCO specifically what observations they have made and what their graduated approach includes.
My child's GP refused to refer. What are my options?
Your GP cannot refuse a referral without giving a clinical reason in writing. Request this in writing. If they maintain their refusal, you can ask to see a different GP, make a formal complaint, or self-refer to some community paediatric services directly. Lumio generates a refusal response letter you can use.

The initial assessment is completely free. No login required. No payment. The assessment takes 13 questions and under 15 minutes. Lumio Plus and Clinical subscriptions are available after the assessment for families who want ongoing clinical support, monthly SLT sessions and weekly progress reports.
Is Lumio Pathways a diagnosis service?
No. Lumio Pathways is not a diagnostic service and does not replace clinical assessment. It provides a clinically validated profile of your child's strengths and needs to help you understand their experience, support them at home and communicate effectively with professionals. A formal diagnosis requires a qualified clinician.
